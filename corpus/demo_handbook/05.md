# Inventory and stock counts

This section covers how parts inventory is tracked, how often it is counted, and what happens when the count disagrees with the record.

## Stock records

Every part carries a stock number. Movements in and out of the parts store are recorded at the moment they happen, by the person making the movement, using the terminal at the store entrance.

Taking a part without recording the movement is the single most common cause of count discrepancies. Where a part is taken urgently and recorded later, the record carries the actual time of removal rather than the time of entry.

## Reorder points

Each part has a reorder point and a reorder quantity. When the recorded stock falls to or below the reorder point, the store generates a purchase request automatically. The request follows the ordinary purchase approval bands.

Reorder points are reviewed twice a year against the previous twelve months of consumption. Parts with no movement for eighteen months are proposed for retirement, and the proposal goes to the head of engineering.

## Counting

A full physical count runs once a year, in the last week of January. The store closes for the duration. Movements during a full count are queued and applied after the count is closed.

Cycle counts run monthly on a rotating subset covering roughly one twelfth of the stock numbers, so that every part is counted at least once between full counts. High-value parts, meaning those above 250 EUR per unit, are cycle counted quarterly rather than annually.

## Discrepancies

A discrepancy is a difference between the counted quantity and the recorded quantity. Discrepancies within one unit or two percent of the recorded quantity, whichever is larger, are adjusted directly by the store keeper.

Larger discrepancies are investigated before any adjustment. The investigation reviews the movement log for the period since the previous count and reports to the head of engineering within five working days. Adjustments above 1,000 EUR in value additionally require the finance director's approval.
