# Reporting an incident

This section defines what counts as an incident, how quickly it is reported, and who reviews it.

## What counts as an incident

An incident is any event that caused harm, damage or loss, and any event that could plausibly have done so. The second category matters as much as the first: an event that caused no harm this time carries the same information about the underlying weakness.

Four categories are recorded.

- Safety: injury, or an event that could have injured someone.
- Equipment: damage, malfunction, or unplanned downtime longer than four hours.
- Security: unauthorised access, a lost badge used after loss, or a suspected data exposure.
- Quality: a defect reaching a customer, or found after a batch was released.

## Reporting deadlines

A safety incident involving injury is reported immediately, by voice, to the site manager or whoever is acting in that role. Every other incident is reported by the end of the working day on which it was noticed.

Reports are filed by whoever noticed the event. Reporting is never delegated upward on the grounds that a manager will describe it better. A manager may add to a report and may not replace it.

## What a report contains

A report states what happened, when, where, who was present, and what was done immediately afterwards. It does not assign blame, and reports that name a person as a cause are returned for rewriting.

Where the reporter has a view on why the event happened, that view is recorded as a separate marked observation, so that later review can distinguish what was seen from what was inferred.

## Review

Safety and security incidents are reviewed within two working days. Equipment and quality incidents are reviewed weekly in a single session.

The review produces either a corrective action with a named owner and a due date, or a written statement that no action is warranted with the reason. Both outcomes close the report. An open corrective action past its due date is raised at the next review.
