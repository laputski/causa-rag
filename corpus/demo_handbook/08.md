# Changing an approved procedure

This section describes how a procedure in this handbook is changed, and what happens between proposal and adoption.

## Who may propose a change

Anyone may propose a change. Proposals are not filtered by seniority, and a proposal from the person who performs the procedure daily carries at least the weight of one from the person who wrote it.

A proposal states the current text, the proposed text, and the problem the change solves. A proposal that describes only a problem is accepted as an observation and is routed to the procedure's owner to draft the text.

## Review

Each procedure has a named owner. The owner reviews a proposal within ten working days and records one of three outcomes: adopt, adopt with changes, or decline with a stated reason.

A declined proposal stays on record with its reason. Where the same problem is raised again by a different person, the earlier decline is reviewed rather than repeated, because a repeated report is evidence that the original reasoning was incomplete.

Changes affecting safety, security or a financial threshold additionally require the department head's agreement before adoption.

## Adoption and effective date

An adopted change takes effect on a stated date, never immediately on approval. The minimum notice is five working days, extended to twenty working days where the change requires training.

The effective date is published together with the change, so that anyone following the old text knows exactly when it stops applying.

## Version history

Every procedure carries a version number and a change log listing, for each version, the effective date, a one-line summary of what changed and the name of the owner who adopted it.

Superseded versions are retained for three years. An incident investigation reads the version that was in force on the date of the event, which is the reason the effective date is recorded rather than the approval date.

## Emergency changes

Where following the current text would create immediate danger, the site manager may suspend a procedure on the spot. A suspension is temporary, lasts at most ten working days, and starts the ordinary change process on the day it is declared.
